# A note to share

Hey mate — I made a little interactive solar-system experience and thought you might enjoy it, or find a place for it in your education content.

You start with Earth about the size of a coin on your phone. The worlds and the distances between them use the same scale, so the empty space becomes part of the experience. You can swipe, zoom and jump between planets, with images, facts and a little reflection on how enormous it all is.

Try it here: https://dougwhite.github.io/scale-of-the-solar-system/

It's free to use, works on phones and computers, and doesn't need a login. The code and original text are open source under MIT, so you're welcome to adapt them; the included images have their own credits and reuse terms, documented in the repo.

Source and download: https://github.com/dougwhite/scale-of-the-solar-system

I'd love to hear whether it sparks any ideas for your learners.

## Three possible classroom prompts

1. Before moving, predict how many screenfuls separate Earth and Mars. Compare the prediction with the experience.
2. Fit the Sun on screen, then return to Earth without changing zoom. Discuss why most textbook diagrams use different scales for size and distance.
3. Compare a manual swipe with an automatic journey. Which parts of the model preserve physical scale, and which parts make a compromise for human attention?

## Context for educators

This is a one-dimensional scale illustration, not an orbital simulation. Positions use approximate mean orbital distances. Automatic travel time is compressed; background stars and locator markers are illustrative. Planet X is explicitly hypothetical. The model stops well inside the wider reaches of the solar system. See the in-app notes and source references for details.
